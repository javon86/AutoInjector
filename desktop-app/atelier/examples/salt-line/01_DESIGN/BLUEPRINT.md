# BOOK BLUEPRINT — The Salt Line

## IDENTITY
- working_title: The Salt Line
- genre / subgenre: literary fiction · coastal drama, slow-burn mystery spine
- audience: adult literary
- comp_titles: *Reservoir 13* meets *The Salt Path*
- tone: restrained, tidal, unsentimental
- style: plain sentences, concrete nouns, weather used sparingly
- narrative_perspective: third limited — Marla Vane
- tense: past
- target_word_count: 15,000
- chapter_count: 12

## CORE
- premise: A surveyor inherits her father's job marking the boundary of poisoned
  coastal soil, and discovers he falsified it for a decade to buy families time.
- story_question: Will Marla publish the true line?
- theme_primary: **mercy vs. accuracy** — stated as a tension, not a topic
- theme_secondary: what we inherit that we did not agree to
- promise_to_reader: the line will be walked to its end and a number will be
  published; the cost of that number will be shown, not summarised.

## CONFLICT
- protagonist_want: to do the survey correctly and be believed
- protagonist_need: to stop arbitrating her father's character and choose for herself
- antagonistic_force: not a person — the sea, the arithmetic, and the town's need
  for the lie to hold one more year
- central_conflict: an accurate line ruins people; a merciful line ruins the coast
- stakes_external: four farms, the harbour grant, the town's flood insurance
- stakes_internal: whether Marla can be her father's daughter without being his accomplice
- stakes_escalation_path: one wrong stake → a decade of them → the harbour grant
  depends on the false figure → publishing implicates her father posthumously

## SHAPE
- beginning_state: Marla arrives to walk the line alone for the first time
- inciting_incident: Ch2 — a stake at Cobb's fence is 40 m seaward of where the
  1994 field book says it should be
- first_turn: Ch4 — the discrepancy is not an error; it is consistent, annual, deliberate
- midpoint: Ch6 — the 1994 field book shows her father's *original* correct line
  and a second set of figures in the same hand
- low_point: Ch9 — Marla publishes an interim figure; Cobb's family loses the field
  and the town turns; she learns her father did it first for the Cobbs
- final_escalation: Ch10-11 — the harbour grant hearing; her figure or his
- climax: Ch11 — Marla submits the true line, with her father's original 1994
  figures attached, naming what he did and why
- resolution: Ch12 — the stakes go in at the true line. Two farms are gone. Marla
  is retained for next spring's survey.
- ending_state: the line is honest and shorter; Marla stays; the town endures, diminished

## CAST ARCS
- **Marla Vane**: dutiful → complicit-by-inheritance → deciding → accountable
- **Ivor Cobb**: neighbourly → betrayed → the one who tells her to publish
- **Dr. Ellen Sarai** (harbour board): procedural → obstructive → useful

## WORLD
- world_overview: an unnamed North Atlantic coast; salt intrusion from a 30-year
  sea-level rise; survey law requires an annual line, filed by 1 June
- hard_rules:
  - the salt line only moves landward; it has never receded
  - a stake is legal evidence only if witnessed and dated
  - the harbour grant requires a filed line by 1 June
- soft_rules:
  - the town treats the survey as ceremony, not enforcement
  - field books are private until filed

## MYSTERY & CRAFT
- major_mysteries: why the stakes are wrong · whether her father acted alone
- reveals: Ch2 discrepancy · Ch4 pattern · Ch6 second figures · Ch9 the Cobbs came first
- foreshadowing_requirements: the lighter (Ch1) · the 1994 field book (Ch1, unopened)
  · Cobb's fence mentioned before it matters (Ch1)
- tonal_boundaries: no supernatural cause · no villain · no twist that invalidates
  an earlier chapter · no weather standing in for emotion
